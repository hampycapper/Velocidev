  ---
  Why GPS Doppler is Accurate Enough

  GPS receivers measure velocity using the Doppler shift of satellite signals, not by differentiating position. A modern
   u-blox module reports velocity accuracy typically ±0.05 m/s (~0.1 knots). That's more than sufficient — you can
  detect a 0.2 knot speed change reliably. At 1Hz updates this is well within your "second or two" window.

  Display recommendation: Go with Option B (7-segment LED) for your first build. It's simpler to wire, far more readable

  trend arrow, etc., but adds complexity.

  ---
  Software (Arduino IDE + ESP32)

  Libraries needed:
  - TinyGPS++ — parses NMEA sentences, extracts SOG (speed over ground) in knots/mph/kmh
  - TM1637Display (if 7-segment) or Adafruit GFX + driver (if TFT)

  Core logic is ~80 lines:
  1. Read GPS UART at 9600 baud
  2. Parse $GNRMC or $GPVTG sentence for SOG
  3. Convert to knots (standard for sailing)
  4. Display; optionally track max speed in session
  5. Optional: log to SD card or send over BT to phone

  The u-blox module can be configured via UBX protocol to run at 5Hz if you want faster updates — straightforward over
  UART.

  ---
  Phases

  Phase 1 — Breadboard prototype (~1 weekend)
  Wire GPS + ESP32 + display on breadboard, confirm you're getting accurate speed readings outdoors.

  Phase 2 — Enclosure + power (~1 weekend)
  Mount in waterproof box, add LiPo + charger, run cable gland for external GPS antenna (optional — internal patch
  antenna works fine through ABS plastic).

  Phase 3 — Boat mounting
  A small RAM mount arm or suction cup base. For a dinghy, a clamp to the thwart or mast base works well.

  ---
  Verdict

  DIY build: ~$80 for a purpose-built, waterproof, glanceable speed display that shows knots to one decimal place.

  Best commercial alternative for your use case: Velocitek ProStart at ~$210 — it's widely used in dinghy racing,
  waterproof, and designed for exactly this. It also has a countdown timer for race starts which is useful.

  The honest tradeoff: DIY saves ~$130 but costs 2–3 weekends of build time, and the commercial unit will have a
  polished enclosure and mount. If you want to add features (heel sensor, VMG, BT data logging to a phone), the DIY
  route becomes clearly better value. If you just want speed readout reliably, the Velocitek is probably worth the
  premium.
